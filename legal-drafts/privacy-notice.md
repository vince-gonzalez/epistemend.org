## What this service does

Epistemend reads what public scholarly indexes record about a body of
published work, and reports where those records agree and where they do
not. It does not host, alter, rank or grade anything. Every finding names
the index it came from.

## The two kinds of people whose data we handle

This notice distinguishes them throughout, because their data reaches us
by different routes and is kept for different reasons.

**Customers** — people who place an order.
**Subjects** — people whose published record is examined by a report. A
subject is often also the customer, but not always.

## Subjects

If a report examines your published record, this section is addressed to
you, and it is our notice under **Article 14** — the provision that applies
where personal data was not obtained from the person it concerns.

### Where the data comes from

Everything in a report is read from public scholarly indexes at the moment
the report is run. We hold no standing database of researchers. The
sources are:

ORCID · Crossref · DataCite · OpenAlex · Europe PMC · PubMed ·
OpenLibrary · Zenodo · Wikidata · Software Heritage · arXiv · doi.org ·
Retraction Watch (via Crossref)

### What categories of data that involves

Name and any former names recorded by those indexes; ORCID identifier;
publication list; co-authors; venues; affiliations where an index records
them; citation counts; deposits; retraction notices; and Wikidata items
naming the person.

We do not knowingly process special category data under Article 9. Where
an index happens to record something that would fall within Article 9, we
neither seek it nor report it.

### Lawful basis, and the balance we struck

Our basis is **Article 6(1)(f)**, legitimate interests. The interest is
the accuracy of the public scholarly record, which is a recognised
interest and is served by identifying where records disagree.

We consider this proportionate because the data is already published by
the subject or their publisher in a professional capacity, was published
precisely so that it would be found, is reported without alteration and
with each source named, and is not combined with any private source. A
Legitimate Interests Assessment supporting this is held and is available
on request.

### Why we do not notify each subject individually

Article 14(5)(b) relieves a controller of individual notification where it
would involve disproportionate effort, provided the information is made
publicly available instead. A single report may touch hundreds of
co-authors and cited authors, none of whom is known to us before the
report runs and none of whom we retain contact details for. **This notice
is the publication that Article 14(5)(b) requires.**

### What we keep about you

Ordinarily, nothing beyond the report itself, which is held for the
customer who bought it. We do not build a profile, we do not retain the
indexes we read, and a subsequent report re-reads the sources afresh.

Where a customer has taken a **monitoring subscription**, we additionally
retain the ORCID identifier being monitored and the previous report, so
that the next run can report what changed. That is the only circumstance
in which we hold data about a subject over time. See "Monitoring" below.

### Your rights

You may:

- **Object** to this processing under Article 21. An objection to
  processing based on legitimate interests requires no reason to be given,
  and we ask for none. We hold a standing list of objections, so an
  objection persists rather than lapsing at the next request: no report
  about you can be ordered, and any monitoring of you stops and its stored
  snapshot is deleted. We will ask enough to satisfy ourselves the request
  comes from you, since otherwise anyone could silence anyone.

  This does not stop your published work appearing in a bibliography that
  somebody else submits for a reference check. That check is about their
  documents rather than about you, and Article 21 protects against
  processing, not against being cited.
- **Access** what we hold about you (Article 15).
- **Rectify** it (Article 16) — though note that where a report is
  inaccurate because an *index* is inaccurate, the correction has to be
  made at the index, and we will tell you which one and how.
- **Erase** it (Article 17).
- **Complain** to your supervisory authority. In the UK that is the
  Information Commissioner's Office; in the EU, your national authority.

Write to **hello@epistemend.org**. We respond within one month.

## Monitoring subscriptions

A monitoring subscription re-runs a check on a schedule and reports what
has changed since the previous run. This necessarily involves retaining,
between runs:

- the ORCID identifier being monitored,
- the previous report, against which the next is compared,
- the customer's email address.

We hold nothing else, and the retention ends when the subscription ends.

If you are the subject of a monitoring subscription and you object under
Article 21, the monitoring stops and the retained snapshot is deleted —
including where the subscriber is someone other than you.

## What we do not do

We do not sell data. We do not share reports with anyone but the customer
who ordered one. We run no advertising, no analytics, and no tracking on
this site — there is nothing here to record a visit with. We do not use
personal data to train any model.

## Security

Reports are reachable only at an address containing a 128-bit random
token, which is never sent to our servers in a way that would place it in
a log, and is not guessable. Anyone holding the address can read that
report, so treat it as you would the document itself.
