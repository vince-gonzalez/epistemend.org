The research behind the papers

Each paper comes out of a standing program. These are the questions the work keeps returning to, and the tools built to answer them.

Vince Gonzalez · ORCID 0009-0005-3640-014X

Axiom hygiene

A proof assistant records what a theorem reaches, not what it needs. The axiom-hygiene program measures the difference — what a machine-checked result actually rests on, read from the source rather than assumed — and removes the assumptions a theorem was paying for and did not use.

The dominator analysis over 766,564 constants found that reachability overstates responsibility by up to 58×, and that 60.1% of classically dependent theorems have no single constant responsible for the dependence at all. In Lean's Mathlib the removable classical dependence has a measured ceiling of 13.1%, and an avoidable classical dependency sits inside omega itself.

The instrument is gonzalgo, an axiom-provenance auditor for Lean 4 and Metamath; the repairs it finds are contributed upstream.

set.mm contributions

Where the analysis finds an avoidable axiom use, the construction half writes the replacement proof and verifies it. Those repairs are merged into metamath/set.mm and the Metamath executable, removing a dependence rather than only reporting it.

Certified bounds in discrete geometry

Machine-checked upper and lower bounds for covering, packing and opacity problems — results where a published bound has to survive someone re-running it on different hardware. The arithmetic is carried in a certified enclosure rather than a float, so a computed inequality becomes a proof. The deposited results include a certified upper bound for the Fejes Tóth point-goalie problem at n = 4 and the maximality of the added-vector codes in the Cohn–Li kissing construction.

Integer sequences (OEIS)

Sequences contributed to the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences under the name Vincent Gonzalez: A398262 published, with A398309 and A398310 in review, each linked to the paper it comes from.

The Kernel Index

A standing, machine-readable measurement of what fourteen formal libraries across six foundations rest on — recomputed rather than quoted, and published CC BY so the numbers can be checked against the libraries themselves.

The business — software, websites and the tools that check them — is at f-keys.com.
